The plan

Three phases, each building on the last

Phase 1 ships a working editor with the three AI tools that cost least and help most. The differentiator — face mapping — arrives in phase 2, onto machinery that is already running in production rather than being built alongside it.

The key idea

AI tools come in two kinds, and it decides almost everything

Some tools can tell us what to do — where to cut, what was said, which colour profile fits. Others have to rebuild the picture itself. Those two things cost different amounts, take different hardware, and leave the user with different amounts of control. Sorting every tool into the right one is the single most consequential decision in the design.

Why this saves real money

Graphics cards are rented by the hour whether they are working or not. By putting every phase 1 tool on the top path, we launch without renting any — and the cluster arrives in phase 2 alongside the one feature that genuinely needs it.

The system

What talks to what

The part worth noticing: video files never pass through our application. The browser uploads straight to storage and plays straight from the delivery network. Only small control messages — "make me a project", "run captions on this clip" — touch our servers.

Why it feels instant

Two copies of every video, doing two different jobs

When someone uploads footage, we immediately make a small, light copy of it. That copy is what they scrub, cut and preview — which is why editing responds instantly and costs us nothing. The full-quality original is touched exactly once more: at export.

How it pays for itself

We show videos, we count credits

Tiers are advertised as "≈30 videos a month" because that is what a creator understands. Underneath, everything is metered in credits — because a literal video counter breaks the first time someone re-runs captions on a clip they have not finished.

Where the margin is comfortable

On phase 1 tools — captions, trimming, grading, export — a ten-minute video costs us roughly $0.10–0.15 to process. Pro at $19.99 for thirty videos leaves plenty of room.

Where it needs watching

Face mapping runs on graphics cards and is an order of magnitude more expensive. That is precisely why it has its own metered allowance instead of sharing the main balance.

These are estimates until we measure them

The cost figures above are calculated, not observed. Cost-per-job is instrumented from the first deployment, so the tiers get re-priced on evidence rather than arithmetic — and because plans live in a database table, changing a price is a data change, not a release.

Where we stand

Nothing is blocking. Building can start.

Every decision the architecture depends on has an answer. What remains has a known default and is needed later, not now.

1

Open the Stripe and Razorpay accounts

Both providers go live at launch, and both need business verification before they will process a real payment. Razorpay additionally needs Indian entity documentation.

Not blocking today — billing is built and tested against test keys. But the lead time is external and nobody on the team controls it, so the applications should start now rather than when the code is ready.

2

Smart trimming: tighten a recording, or cut it down to its best parts?

Removing silences and stumbles saves roughly 10–25% of a recording. The original spec promises ten minutes down to two, which means choosing what is worth keeping — a substantially harder tool.

Affects what we promise publicly, not what gets built first. Needed before the feature is described to customers.

3

Storage retention, and who owns tax

How long we keep uploads is the largest recurring cost in the system and the only one that grows on its own. Indian GST and EU VAT both need configuring — the providers offer tools, but choosing and setting them up is not a development task.

Both needed before launch, neither blocks building.

One thing to keep in view

Phase 1 ships without face mapping, so it cannot be marketed as the face-swap product — its story is "the editor that does the boring work for you". That is the right build order, but if launch is being sold on the differentiator, it is a conversation worth having now rather than in three months.
